Her story

Thalia was born and raised on the Navajo reservation. She is of the Water Flows Together People, Tó'áhání. Born for the Coyote Pass People, Naaneesht'ézhí Tábąąhá. She was raised with a rich Navajo culture, herding sheep with her late maternal grandmother on her family's homeland. For much of her childhood she came to understand adaptation through the stories of the Long Walk (Hwéeldi) passed down from her late grandmother, and through her own experience of limited running water and electricity, and learning her late mother's variations on Navajo foods for family dinners.

She began work in Phoenix, Arizona after high school, holding corporate jobs until she followed a calling to pursue her long-time fascination with scones. After culinary school, she started her business, Scone On?, in 2019.

Her business managed to flourish during the pandemic, and she made a name for herself as the "Scone Lady." In recent years she has expanded into other corn-based pastries — cakes, cookies, breads, and seasonings.

While she loves making pastries, she has also found a growing passion in speaking engagements, sharing her knowledge and preparing the next generation. She has worked with schools on the reservation to encourage and instill Indigenous food sovereignty, dedicating much of 2023 and 2024 to bringing communities together — on the reservation, with non-profit organizations, and on national television. Her work continues normalizing Indigenous foods to help address the health crises facing generations before and after us.
